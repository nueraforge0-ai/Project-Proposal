### 1.2 Statement of the Problem

Nkumba University's Security Unit receives found property at the main gate, but the arrangement depends on two things: that the finder is honest enough to hand the item in, and that the owner thinks to come and ask. The Security Unit holds the item and waits for a claim, and nothing is done to tell the owner that it has been found. For students in hostels or distant lecture blocks, checking costs time, and some owners may not try at all if they assume the item is gone.

The team has not seen a written record of each item, a standard way of checking ownership before release, or a rule on how long unclaimed items are kept **[CONFIRM]**. This project does not assume answers to these points. Establishing them is the first specific objective (Section 1.3).

The risk is higher for some items. Identity cards and phones contain personal information, so releasing them needs a reliable way of checking ownership (Uganda, 2019). To our knowledge, no consolidated record exists. The university therefore cannot easily say how many items are found, how many are claimed, which items go missing most often, or where and when. Without such evidence, administrators cannot plan for the problem.

The campus-level systems reviewed in Section 1.1 were built for Malaysian universities, apart from the Makerere mobile application (Itaagi, 2022). In the sources we reviewed, none describes a system designed around gate-based custody, officer verification, AI-assisted matching and administrator analytics for a Ugandan university.

The problem is therefore the lack of a centralised, searchable and verification-based lost and found system at Nkumba University that (a) records every found item when Security receives it, (b) lets owners report and search for items from their phones, (c) suggests possible matches for staff to confirm, and (d) gives administrators summaries of what is lost, found and unclaimed.

The problem affects everyone who uses the main campus: students from all seven schools (Nkumba University, n.d.), staff and visitors, including hostel residents. Nkumba University serves several thousand students **[VERIFY: registrar's enrolment figure]**. The project also fits Uganda's Fourth National Development Plan, whose Digital Transformation Programme aims to raise the use of digital services and strengthen data protection (National Planning Authority, 2026).
