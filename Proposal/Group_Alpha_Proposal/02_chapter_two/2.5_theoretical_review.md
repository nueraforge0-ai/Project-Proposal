### 2.5 Theoretical Review

#### 2.5.1 Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)

The Technology Acceptance Model explains the use of a technology mainly through perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use (Davis, 1989), and it continues to inform studies of technology acceptance in higher education (Kampa, 2023). It is relevant here because students and officers will only benefit from the system if they are willing to use it. A system that lets an owner check for a lost item from a phone may be seen as useful, and clear forms and simple navigation may make it easy to use. TAM therefore supports the evaluation of the prototype with users, Security Officers and Administrators (Specific Objective 3 and Research Question 4). Because TAM addresses acceptance and not technical quality, the evaluation also considers functionality, reliability and security.

#### 2.5.2 Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)

UTAUT combines several earlier acceptance models into four determinants of behavioural intention: performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence and facilitating conditions (Venkatesh et al., 2003). Systematic reviews in higher education confirm that performance expectancy and effort expectancy remain consistent predictors of acceptance (Xue et al., 2024). UTAUT is relevant because the system will be used by people whose expectations and support differ. Performance expectancy asks whether users believe the system helps them recover items, effort expectancy asks how easy it is to learn, social influence reflects the effect of peers and the Security Unit, and facilitating conditions cover devices, connectivity and training. The two theories complement each other: TAM informs the usefulness and ease-of-use items in the evaluation, and UTAUT adds the social and organisational conditions that may affect adoption at Nkumba University.
