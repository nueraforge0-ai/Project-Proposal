### 2.7 Summary of Reviewed Studies and Identified Gaps

| **Author/Year** | **Setting and focus** | **Contribution** | **Gap relevant to this study** |
|----|----|----|----|
| Tan & Chong (2023) | UKM, Malaysia. Web and Android lost-and-found service (FoundeLost) | Login with university email and matric number, listing with photographs, search, mandatory security questions, automatic deletion after a month | Users post items and arrange the handover themselves, with no custody point. No AI matching or analytics. The authors list analytics, campus-security integration and machine-learning matching as future work |
| Murugiah & Mat Nawi (2025) | UTHM, Malaysia. Web application (FindItHere) | Matching of lost and found reports, real-time notifications, administrator dashboard | The abstract does not describe officer-led custody or release, or AI-generated summaries |
| Ngu (2025) | UNIMAS, Malaysia. AI-driven app (FindIt) | Image matching, notifications, status tracking, administrator web interface for claims and accounts | Built as a mobile application around image matching. The abstract does not describe a custody-point workflow or trend analytics for administrators |
| Twayigize (2025) | Strathmore University, Kenya. AI-driven web system | Multimodal image-text matching with CLIP from user reports | The abstract describes user reporting and matching, and not Security-held custody, claim verification or administrator analytics |
| Itaagi (2022) | Makerere University, Uganda. Mobile application (SearchiT) | Mobile application for finding lost items, based on a student survey | The abstract does not describe AI, officer verification or administrator analytics. It is a mobile application, not a PWA |
| Troov (described by Tan & Chong, 2023) | Commercial worldwide service | Matching algorithm, notifications and a security question to prove ownership | Designed for the whole world and not for one campus or one custody point |

#### Gap Analysis

The table shows four gaps. First, almost all the systems reviewed let users post items and arrange the handover themselves. None of them is described as starting from the officer who physically receives the item, which is how property reaches Nkumba University's main gate. Second, ownership checks in the reviewed systems rely on security questions set by the finder or the system. A Security Officer who holds the item is better placed to verify a claim, because the officer can compare the claimant's details with a record made at the time of receipt. Third, the AI systems reviewed (Ngu, 2025; Twayigize, 2025) concentrate on matching, and the abstracts we found do not describe analytics for administrators, which Tan and Chong (2023) themselves identify as future work. Fourth, only one of the Ugandan or regional sources reviewed concerns a Ugandan university (Itaagi, 2022), and we found no description of a PWA built for Ugandan connectivity and data-cost conditions.

#### Unique Contribution

This project builds a lost and found system around the way property is actually held at Nkumba University: at the main gate, by the Security Unit. The digital record starts with the officer who receives the item, ownership is verified by that officer before release, and AI is limited to suggesting matches and summarising records for staff to review. In the sources we reviewed, no system combines officer-led custody, human-verified AI matching and administrator analytics in a lightweight PWA for a Ugandan university.
