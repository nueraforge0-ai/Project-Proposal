### 3.2 Research Design

#### 3.2.1 Case Study Design

The study uses a descriptive case study design with Nkumba University as the case. A case study suits the project because it needs detailed information about one organisation: how found items are received at the main gate, how owners find out about them, how claims are handled, and what a system must provide to fit the Security Unit's way of working. The study does not aim to generalise to all universities. It aims to describe the situation at Nkumba University in depth and to design a system suited to it, then to build and evaluate a working prototype.

Other designs were considered and not adopted. Experimental and longitudinal designs would need control groups or repeated measurement over a long period, which is not realistic within the time of a student project. A purely correlational design would not produce the working system that the project requires.

#### 3.2.2 Research Approach

The study adopts a mixed-methods approach (Creswell & Creswell, 2023). A qualitative approach is used to understand current practice and the information officers need, because these involve procedures and experiences that are not easily expressed as numbers. A quantitative approach is used to measure users' views on the functionality, usability, reliability, security and perceived usefulness of the prototype, with structured questionnaires and descriptive statistics. Combining the two lets the study cross-check findings from different sources. Table 3.1 shows how each specific objective will be addressed.

**Table 3.1: Link between study objectives, methods and analysis**

| **Specific objective (Chapter One)** | **Data sources** | **Methods and instruments** | **Analysis** |
|----|----|----|----|
| 1\. Investigate how lost and found property is handled and establish requirements | Students, Security Officers, ICT Directorate staff, the main gate procedure, existing records | Questionnaire, interview guide, direct observation at the main gate, document review | Descriptive statistics; thematic analysis; requirements specification |
| 2\. Design and develop the system with role-based interfaces and AI features | Requirements specification; feedback from officers, administrators and users | Iterative prototyping; UML and database design; feedback sessions | Testing against requirements; functional testing; feedback review |
| 3\. Test and evaluate the system | Students, Security Officers and Administrators | Test cases, AI matching test, evaluation questionnaire (SUS, TAM and UTAUT items), brief follow-up interviews | Descriptive statistics; thematic analysis |
