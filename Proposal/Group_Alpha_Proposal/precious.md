2.9 Synthesis of the Literature

The reviewed literature generally indicates that maintaining searchable digital records can improve the process of identifying and recovering lost property compared with relying entirely on informal communication and manual enquiries. However, the reviewed studies differ in their areas of emphasis. Tan and Chong (2023) focus on ease of item submission and searching, Murugiah and Mat Nawi (2025) emphasise item matching and notifications, while Ngu (2025) and Twayigize (2025) examine the application of artificial intelligence in matching items using image and textual information.

The proposed study does not intend to duplicate these existing systems but rather builds on their relevant approaches to address the specific requirements of Nkumba University. The proposed system adopts relevant features demonstrated in the reviewed studies, including structured item records, photographs, search facilities, notifications and item-matching techniques, and adapts them to a university environment in which recovered property is received and managed by authorised Security personnel.

In addition, the proposed system will incorporate ownership verification by authorised Security personnel before property release, AI-assisted analytical summaries for administrators and measures to protect users' personal information in accordance with Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 (Uganda, 2019). These features will be developed to address the requirements identified in the literature review and the operational needs of the university.

Furthermore, the study will adopt a practical development approach appropriate for an undergraduate project, prioritising the implementation of essential system functionalities before incorporating AI-assisted features as additional enhancements.
