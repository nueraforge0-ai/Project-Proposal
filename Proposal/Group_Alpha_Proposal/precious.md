3.11 Anticipated Limitations

First, the study will involve a relatively small sample, and convenience sampling will be used to select student respondents. This may introduce selection bias and limit the extent to which the findings can be generalised beyond Nkumba University. To address this limitation, the study will clearly describe the characteristics of the selected respondents and interpret the findings within the specific context of Nkumba University.

Second, the study will rely partly on self-reported information from participants, which may be incomplete, inaccurate or influenced by personal opinions and the desire to provide favourable responses. To minimise this limitation, anonymous questionnaires will be used where appropriate, and information will be collected through multiple methods, including interviews, observation and document review, to support the comparison of findings.

Third, some participants, particularly Security personnel, may be reluctant to provide detailed information about existing property-handling procedures due to confidentiality or institutional concerns. The researcher will explain the purpose of the study, assure participants of appropriate confidentiality and obtain the necessary permissions before collecting such information.

Fourth, limitations in time, financial resources and access to institutional facilities may restrict the extent of system development and deployment. Consequently, the study will focus on developing and evaluating a functional prototype rather than implementing a fully operational university-wide system. As a result, the study may not establish the system's long-term performance, sustainability or effectiveness under continuous real-world operating conditions.

Fifth, variations in internet connectivity, network availability and user devices may affect the system's testing results and user experience. The study will therefore document the devices, browsers and network conditions used during system evaluation.

Finally, the evaluation of the AI-assisted matching functionality will rely on simulated lost-item claims and registered found-item records. Therefore, the results will demonstrate the behaviour of the matching method under controlled testing conditions but may not fully represent its accuracy and performance when applied to actual lost-and-found records. These limitations will be acknowledged in the final report, together with recommendations for further research and improvements to the proposed system.
