3.1 Introduction

This chapter presents the research methods and procedures that will be employed in the design, development and evaluation of a web-based Lost and Found Property Management System with AI Analytics at Nkumba University. It describes the research design and approach, study area, target population, sample size and sampling techniques, data collection methods and instruments, system development methodology, testing and evaluation procedures, data analysis methods, ethical considerations and limitations of the study.

The methodology is guided by the research objectives presented in Chapter One and the findings from the literature reviewed in Chapter Two. Specifically, the study seeks to examine the existing procedures for handling lost and found property at Nkumba University, design and develop the proposed system with its AI-assisted functionalities, and evaluate its performance and usability. The literature reviewed in Chapter Two identified limitations in existing systems, particularly regarding user-based item registration, ownership verification and the provision of analytical information for administrators.

The selected methods will first establish the existing procedures and challenges involved in receiving, recording, storing and releasing found property at the university, and will then guide the development and evaluation of a system suited to the identified requirements. Consistent with the theoretical foundations discussed in Chapter Two, the system evaluation will be informed by the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), particularly in assessing users' perceptions, acceptance and willingness to use the proposed system.
