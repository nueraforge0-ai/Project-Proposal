2.10 Chapter Summary

This chapter reviewed literature on the management of lost and found property in universities, existing digital lost-and-found systems in Malaysia, Kenya and Uganda, ownership verification, item matching and artificial intelligence, system security and data protection, and usability and mobile accessibility. The reviewed literature indicates that digital lost-and-found systems are feasible and can improve access to information about recovered property. However, the studies reviewed reveal limitations in their approaches to item registration, ownership verification and administrative analytics, particularly in relation to systems that depend on user-submitted listings rather than registration by authorised institutional personnel.

The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) were adopted as theoretical foundations for understanding and evaluating users' acceptance and use of the proposed system. The conceptual framework illustrates the relationship between the proposed system's features, the intervening factors and the expected improvements in the recovery and management of lost property at Nkumba University.

Overall, the literature reviewed provides a foundation for the study objectives and research questions presented in Chapter One and informs the research methodology discussed in Chapter Three.
